## 2 · `Answerable⟨agent, party, decision, window, refusal, appeal⟩` — built

The corpus named this object and never constructed it. It is the empty origin of its own
2×2 — the OBSERVER, "you", the dative second person.

**The unification this file encodes.** GID090's blocker (verifier independence) and the
orphaned legitimacy layer (§6.1 of the recovered material) are *the same gap*. A second
party is not sufficient for independence: **a verifier that cannot refuse without cost is a
rubber stamp, and a rubber stamp is a second party.** What makes a verifier real is exactly
the consent operator:

```
ð(S,p) = announced(S,p) ∧ refusable(S,p) ∧ ¬penalised(refusal)
CAGE(S,p) ⟺ ¬ð(S,p)          TRELLIS(S,p) ⟺ ð(S,p)
              ⇓
Independent(W, C) ⟸ ð(decision(C), W) ∧ W ≠ constructor
```

Both conjuncts required. Distinctness alone → rubber stamp. ð alone with the same party →
self-certification. 9/9 checks pass, and the discriminating case is the middle one:

| case | party | ð | class | admitted as verifier? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no second party | = constructor | ✗ | CAGE | **no** — loop generated and judged by one party |
| **rubber stamp** | distinct, but refusal costs it | ✗ | **CAGE** | **no** — distinct is not enough |
| real party | distinct, announced, refusal free | ✓ | TRELLIS | **yes** |

The rubber-stamp case fails on the third conjunct alone: a channel exists, the window is

### 3.1 · Deconfounding — and a correction to my own analysis

`apply_party()` changed **two** things: the verifier identity *and* σ_S
(`RESEARCH_ONLY → ALLOW_LIMITED`). That is a confounded intervention, and the corpus's own
law forbids reading a causal estimate off one. Isolated:

```
+verifier_only    1/5 verdicts changed
+steward_only     0/5
+both_of_those    4/5
```

My first classifier printed *"the VERIFIER is the causal variable; stewardship was a
passenger."* **That conclusion is wrong and the numbers refute it.** 1 + 0 ≪ 4 is a
superadditive interaction, not a main effect. Corrected:

> **Neither factor is sufficient. Both are necessary.**
> A verifier with no authority to allow is as inert as authority with no verifier.
> The corpus had neither — which is why the gap was invisible: removing either one alone
> would have looked like it made no difference.

This is the kind of error the harness exists to catch, caught by the harness, on its author.

### 3.2 · What the repairs actually do
